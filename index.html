1. Getting Started

Understanding Zcash Addresses

Address Types: The Foundation

T-Addresses (Transparent)

t1... (35 characters)

  • ❌ No privacy - like Bitcoin
  • ✅ Public blockchain visibility
  • ✅ Exchange compatibility
  • ✅ Mining rewards, hardware wallets
  • ⚠️ Think: Glass bank vault

U-Addresses (Unified)

u1... (141-213 characters)

  • ✅ Full privacy by default
  • ✅ Bundles multiple address types
  • ✅ Auto-selects best privacy option
  • ✅ Future-proof (Orchard + Sapling)
  • 🎯 Primary recommendation!
T-addresses are transparent addresses starting with "t" - they function exactly like Bitcoin addresses with complete public visibility. U-addresses are unified addresses introduced to bundle transparent, Sapling, and Orchard receivers into one "universal adapter". When someone sends to your u-address, their wallet automatically selects the most private option available (Orchard preferred, then Sapling, then transparent). This is the future of Zcash addressing.

When to Use T-Addresses

✅ Use T-Addresses For:

  • 🏦 Exchange deposits/withdrawals (most exchanges require them)
  • ⛏️ Mining rewards and hardware wallet compatibility
  • 🔄 Temporary holding before immediate shielding
  • 💸 Services that don't support shielded addresses yet

⚠️ Critical: Zashi auto-shields after 10 confirmations. Always shield manually ASAP for privacy!

Best practice is the "hybrid strategy": Receive from exchanges via t-address, then immediately shield to u-address. Zashi's auto-shielding feature moves transparent funds to private pools automatically after 10 confirmations, but manual shielding is faster and more secure. Think of t-addresses as a loading dock where you quickly move goods to secure storage.

🔑 What are Viewing Keys?

Cryptographic separation of viewing & spending

  • 👁️ View-only access to shielded transactions
  • 🔒 Can see balances & history, but cannot spend funds
  • 📊 Introduced in Sapling upgrade (2018) via ZIP 310
  • 🛡️ Read-only bank account access without withdrawal rights

Real-world use case: Share with your accountant for tax compliance without risking spending authority

Viewing keys enable critical separation of spending permissions and viewing permissions for Zcash shielded addresses. Imagine giving someone read-only access to your bank account without the ability to withdraw funds. Introduced in the Sapling upgrade (2018) via ZIP 310, they solve the compliance problem without compromising security.

Types of Viewing Keys

Incoming Viewing Key (IVK)

  • Shows only incoming transactions
  • View amounts & memo fields
  • Perfect for payment detection
  • Exchanges use for deposits

💡 Use case: Exchange deposit verification

Full Viewing Key (FVK)

  • Shows incoming & outgoing
  • Complete transaction history
  • All amounts, addresses, memos
  • Ideal for auditors/accountants

💡 Use case: Tax compliance & auditing

⚠️ Cannot be revoked! Treat like sensitive bank statements. Share via encrypted channels only.

IVKs are perfect for exchanges wanting to verify customer deposits without exposing spending keys. Spending keys stay in secure HSMs while the IVK monitors incoming payments. FVKs reveal complete history - ideal for accountants, auditors, or charities proving donation receipts publicly. Critical privacy implication: once shared, viewing keys reveal your complete transaction history for that address and cannot be revoked. Anyone with a viewing key can share it further.

Viewing Keys in Zashi

How to Export:

  1. Open Zashi → Advanced Settings
  2. Navigate to Backup
  3. Export viewing key (IVK or FVK)
  4. Share securely via encrypted channel

Best Practices:

  • 🔐 Use different addresses for different purposes (granular access)
  • 📄 Consider payment disclosures for single-transaction proof
  • 🔒 Share via Signal, PGP, or other encrypted channels
  • ⚠️ Never share on public forums or unencrypted email
In Zashi, export viewing keys via Advanced Settings → Backup. View-only wallet import is coming in future releases. Best practices include using secure encrypted channels for sharing, considering payment disclosures for single-transaction proof instead of full viewing keys, and using different addresses for different purposes to enable granular access control.

RPC Selection & Network Privacy

🌐 Understanding Lightwalletd

RPC servers = lightwalletd services

  • 📡 Stateless backend servers providing bandwidth-efficient blockchain access
  • ⚡ Communicates via gRPC, serves "compact blocks"
  • 💾 90%+ bandwidth savings vs. full node sync
  • 🔐 Your spending keys stay local - servers can't spend funds
  • 🔍 Trial decryption happens on your device to identify transactions

Lightwalletd pre-processes blockchain data from full nodes (zcashd or Zebra), caches it locally, and serves light clients efficiently.

RPC servers in Zcash wallets refer to lightwalletd services - stateless backend servers providing bandwidth-efficient access to the Zcash blockchain. Unlike full nodes requiring the entire blockchain download, Zashi communicates with lightwalletd servers via gRPC, receiving "compact blocks" for 90%+ bandwidth savings. Lightwalletd pre-processes blockchain data from full nodes (zcashd or Zebra), caches it locally, and serves light clients. Zashi holds your spending keys locally and performs trial decryption to identify your transactions - servers cannot spend funds, only provide blockchain state.

Choosing servers involves critical trade-offs. Self-hosted servers offer maximum privacy and control but require technical expertise, full node operation, and hours of initial sync. 🕵️ Why Network-Layer Privacy Matters

Two-layer privacy: Cryptographic + Network

The Problem:

  • 🔍 Zcash's cryptography hides transaction details (amounts, addresses, memos)
  • 📍 But your IP address is visible to RPC servers & network observers
  • 🔗 IP metadata reveals: location, usage patterns, transaction timing
  • ⚠️ Historical vulnerability: CVE-2019-16930 demonstrated these risks

🎯 Privacy requires both cryptographic shielding AND network obfuscation

While Zcash provides world-class cryptographic privacy hiding transaction details, without network-layer protection your IP address becomes a critical fingerprint linking ostensibly private transactions. Network observers can correlate transactions by identifying which node first broadcasts them - revealing identity, location, and usage patterns. Zcash's peer-to-peer layer doesn't natively obfuscate IP addresses. Historical vulnerability CVE-2019-16930 demonstrated these risks. Privacy requires both cryptographic shielding AND network obfuscation.

🧅 Arti: Native Tor in Zashi

Built-in Tor via Rust implementation

$670,000 funding from Zcash Community Grants

What is Arti?

  • 🦀 Pure Rust Tor implementation (memory-safe, mobile-optimized)
  • 📱 Embedded directly in Zashi - no external setup required!
  • 🔒 Routes ALL traffic through Tor's 3-hop circuit
  • 🌐 Covers: transactions, blockchain sync, NEAR Intents swaps, exchange rates

How to Enable:

  1. Open Zashi → Settings → Advanced Settings
  2. Enable Tor Network Protection
  3. That's it! One-click activation
Zashi's Tor integration (beta) was introduced in version 2.1 via Arti - a pure Rust implementation funded by $670,000 from Zcash Community Grants. Enable via Settings → Advanced Settings → Tor Network Protection. No external setup required; Zashi includes the Tor client natively. All traffic - transaction submission, blockchain sync, NEAR Intents swaps, exchange rate fetching - automatically routes through Tor's 3-hop circuit, hiding your IP from all network participants.
